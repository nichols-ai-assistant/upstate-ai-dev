## How They Make Money

INFICON's business model has two distinct revenue layers:

**Hardware revenue (recurring / replacement cycle)**
- Sensors and gauges embedded in manufacturing equipment -- each semiconductor fab has hundreds or thousands of INFICON sensors
- When a fab upgrades or expands a production line, INFICON sensors are typically required
- Refresh/replacement cycle: gauges and RGAs wear out and must be replaced; helium leak detectors are periodically calibrated and serviced
- Stickiness: once a process is characterized around an INFICON sensor, switching creates recertification burden for the fab

**Software revenue (SaaS / license)**
- FabGuard, SmartFDC, FabTime, Factory Scheduler are sold as software licenses or SaaS
- High switching cost -- FabGuard is embedded in fab operations workflows and connected to hundreds of data streams
- FabTime was acquired as a standalone SaaS subscription business (enterprise software model)

**Services revenue**
- Application engineering, calibration, training, support contracts
- The Americas Application Center is partly a vehicle to deepen service relationships

**The integrated play**: INFICON's strategic moat is the sensor-to-software stack. A competitor can sell a cheaper gauge, but they can't easily replicate the FabGuard/SmartFDC layer that sits on top and provides process intelligence. Hardware generates data; software monetizes the data.
