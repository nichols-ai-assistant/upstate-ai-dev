Where most AI decisions start

Rarely with a strategy session. Usually with one of these.

The vendor pitch

Your core or digital banking provider just demoed its new AI features. The demo looked good. Now someone has to decide whether it fits how your institution actually works, and what it will ask of your team.

The quiet adoption

Staff are already using AI tools to draft emails and summarize documents. Nobody has agreed what is fine to paste in, which tools are approved, or who answers questions when they come up.

The stalled pilot

A pilot showed promise. Now it needs an owner, a way to judge whether it worked, and a plan for what happens next. Without those, it sits.

What progress looks like

  • A choice you can explain.Leadership can walk the board and colleagues through why a use case was chosen, what it depends on, and how it will be judged.
  • A team that can run it.The people who use the tool know how it works, where it can go wrong, and who to call.
  • Confidence without the hype.You evaluate the next vendor pitch on your terms, with questions ready, instead of reacting to a demo.
